## 1. The reading rule

The corpus applies to itself a discipline that this laboratory adopts as is:

- **Epistemic labels** — [T] theorem · [CERT] finite certificate · [M] measurement · [DEV] development · [H] hypothesis · [OPEN] open obligation · [FAIL] failure · [R] retracted · [EXT] external validation · [ARCH] result from an archived model · [A] axiom · [DEF] definition · [METH] methodological rule · [R-ALG] inherited algebraic error, corrected.
- **Failures are shown first** and are never reworded to look like successes.
- **No resurrection**: an [R] or [FAIL] result may be told as provenance; it never becomes true again through repetition.
- **No historical [T] enters without direct recomputation.** The armature V347.1 has already found three classes of errors that had passed through hundreds of pages; it assumes that others exist until proven otherwise.
- **Occam's razor**: three independent validation paths (analytical, multi-seed with error bars, cross-validation by three algorithms).
- **Tribunal of eight hostile reviewers** (probabilist, theoretical physicist, philosopher of science, observational cosmologist, topologist, information theorist, Bayesian statistician, experimentalist).

## 2. The framework on one page

The Blob Principle is read as a **mirror**: a cosmogonic *narrative* (Book I) and its *mathematical twin* (Book II), the gap between the two being recorded as a **residue** — CARRIED (*PORTÉ*: the formalism says what the narrative says), WEAKENED (*AFFAIBLI*: it says less), CORRECTED (*CORRIGÉ*: it contradicts it and the narrative yields), ABSENT (*ABSENT*: no object exists yet).

Formally, the corpus contains three strata that must be distinguished:

| Stratum | Objects | Dynamics | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A_gen** | abstract simplicial complexes, lineage σ, mitosis μ, variable N | partial genealogical growth | origin / cascade |
| **A_Gibbs** [ARCH] | Rips/flag graph at fixed N, functional *S_sim(C) = log(1 + n_tri(C)) − βD(C)* | edge exchange + Gibbs measure | historical selection |
| **B** (B_shell, B_full) | finite triangulations of T³, action *S(T) = μN₃ + Σₑ f(nₑ)* | Pachner 2↔3 (and 1↔4) | canonical sector G0 |

The two bridges A_gen → A_Gibbs (RO-25) and A_Gibbs → B (RO-08) **are not built**: the cosmogony and the canonical sector currently form a genealogy of models, not yet a single dynamics derived end to end.

## 4 bis. What the public laboratory has established since opening

These results are entered in the [advances log](ADVANCES_LOG.md). They are checked and replayed by the [AI pilot](AI_PILOT.md), **not reviewed by a person** and **not integrated into the corpus**; **none changes a status** of the programme. They mostly sharpen the growth → Gibbs bridge (RO-25) and closure (RO-24), on abstract graphs unless the canonical engine is mentioned.

- **[T] M − N invariant**: mitosis, leaf collapse and edge swaps conserve M − N; from Ω⁰, M = N − 1 forever. RO-24's "most economical option" (interleaving A_Gibbs swaps between births) is **refuted**: in the M = N − 1 sector every triangle disconnects the graph, and selection produces islands. Any viable closure must create about κ̄/2 − 1 edges per birth (advance no. 2).
- **[T] Genealogical obstruction**: no growth that leaves old edges unrewired reproduces π_N exactly; growth with closure followed by relaxation **derives** π_N at finite N, at the price of the edges' genealogy (advance no. 2).
- **[M] Bridge cost on the canonical engine**: a newborn's degree reaches the mean in 8 to 16 sweeps, with no growth from N = 250 to 4,000; a birth therefore costs work proportional to N (advance no. 3). The newborn's triangle-mass law reaches the population's in 16 to 256 sweeps, except the massive tail, joined slowly (advance no. 5, open challenge DEFI-M10).
- **[T]+[M] Error in the corpus**: the archived V109 engine overcounts triangles by 1.4 to 2.7%; the canonical engine is not affected (advance no. 4).
- **[M] Six inconsistencies between the V346 master and its JSON files**: the N = 1000 row of table M-7.1 "convergence d_s → 3" copies the N = 3000 row; the area-law exponent "σ² ∝ V^≈1" does not match the recorded fit (3.23); a bound in appendix A1.5 is evaluated ten times too high (the sign δ₀ > 0 holds); the simulated cosmic web is not, in its own data, "specific to viability"; two further discrepancies. None of the values at issue is carried over into the V347.1 framework (advance no. 8, open challenge DEFI-R6).
- **[T] The attachment law derived from S_sim**: at a fixed number of edges, Gibbs attachment (anti-preferential triadic closure, no new parameter) is the best possible law at birth. **[CERT]** In the cases computed exactly, it nevertheless slows the subsequent relaxation whenever it beats uniform: being born right is not the right criterion for the bridge (advance no. 6).
- **[CERT] + [M] The bridge's cost is aristocratic**: in the 102 exact cases with at most 7 vertices, the most faithful birth slows relaxation in 50 of the 57 cases where it gains. On abstract graphs from N = 8 to 64, the time a newborn takes to acquire the equilibrium triangle mass grows at least like N³ sweeps, possibly exponentially, almost independently of the birth law: that mass is carried by an aristocracy of vertices. The canonical engine has not yet been tested on this point (advance no. 7, open challenge DEFI-M12).

## 5. Reading caution for the master V346

The audit of 24 September 2026 notes that the master V346 carries **four successive verdicts on SUP-1**, from "open frontier" at the head of the document to "SUP-1 IS CLOSED" in part CLXXVI (18 July 2026). **The most recent is authoritative**, but nothing signals this to the reader who starts on page 1. Four audit points remain open on this verdict:

1. hypothesis (H1) posits a **bounded** degree, whereas its verification provides a degree in **O(log N)**;
2. the order of limits is not specified — at finite N, t → ∞ gives a zero exponent;
3. the theorem lives on a **3-torus**, whereas the v137 engine runs on a **cube with boundary**;
4. the label (H1)–(H3) designates **four different triplets** in the same document.

These four points are an **ideal entry point for a first contributor**.

### The "spectral squeeze" does not close as it stands

The audit of 18 September 2026 ([audits_18-09-2026/](../corpus/02_portes_et_audits/audits_18-09-2026/)) establishes, from Delmotte's own exposition (Séminaire de théorie spectrale et géométrie, Institut Fourier, 1997–1998, openly accessible on Numdam), that the theorem invoked to close **INF-1** requires two hypotheses: a uniform ellipticity A*(α), which imposes a **degree bounded by 1/α**, and a loop at each vertex, p(x,x) ≥ α. Consequences:

- **INF-1 as a closed [T] is not justified as it stands**; its applicability to the corpus is an open question [Q], conditional on uniformly bounded degree, volume doubling and the Poincaré inequality.
- The corpus contains an internal contradiction: one section closes INF-1 via Delmotte, while annexes V232 and V236 withdraw Delmotte (measured non-Ahlfors-regularity, d_w ≈ 2.84). The theorem being an equivalence, the two positions cannot coexist.
- **Tension with SUP-1**: SUP-1 relies on a non-elliptic framework (Procaccia–Rosenthal–Sapozhnikov), INF-1 requires uniform ellipticity. A single object must satisfy both for the spectral squeeze to close: **unresolved**.
- Favourable point: the parity obstruction that the loop serves to bypass is probably benign here, the graphs of the corpus being saturated with triangles.
